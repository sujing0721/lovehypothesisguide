The premise, 10 seconds version

Olive Smith is a fourth-year astrophysics PhD student whose life is caffeinated chaos: a messy grad-school friend group, a wedding she's emotionally unhelpful at, and an ex who shows up unannounced and won't take a hint. When panic-telling goes badly, Olive claims she's seriously dating one of the faculty — Dr. Adam Carlsen, the youngest, most devastatingly handsome, most famously terrifying professor on campus (his students have a running list of people he's made cry).

The part nobody saw coming: Adam agrees to play along, for reasons of his own. The fake relationship starts very real — right up until it can't stay secret that Olive is his student.

Fans of the book will recognize the beats: the coffee-shop deal, the motorcycle, the grumpy-professor-with-a-soft-center act. The novel began life as a Star Wars fanfiction, and that origin story is a big part of why the book (and now the film) carries such a devoted fanbase.

How it's being received

Critics have been mixed in first reviews — the usual pattern for BookTok adaptations, where the material comes with a million pre-formed opinions. What we're seeing from readers and audiences so far skews warmer: Reinhart committed to the role (she's also an executive producer), and the Olive/Adam banter survives the trip to screen. The biggest consistent complaint from the fandom is volume of source material — 380+ pages, three parts and an epilogue compressed into 112 minutes.

The book vs the movie, in one paragraph

The film is markedly tamer than the novel — the book's infamous explicit side-plot (Olive's advisor Simone and her husband Isaac — if you know, you know) does not survive at full strength in an all-ages streamer. The fake-dating spine, the reveal scene beats and the emotional ending stay. Full breakdown in Book vs Movie: every change.

FAQ

Where can I watch The Love Hypothesis?

Exclusively on Amazon Prime Video, worldwide, streaming free with a Prime subscription as of September 23, 2026.

Is it on Netflix?

No — it's an Amazon MGM Studios production, so Prime Video is its only home.

Is there a post-credits scene?

As of the first day of release, no post-credits or mid-credits scene has been widely reported by viewers. We're verifying this and will update the page the moment enough audience reports line up. Last checked: Sep 24, 2026.

Will there be a sequel?

Nothing has been officially greenlit yet. But the source material stacks the deck: Problematic Summer Romance is a companion novel returning this world, Hazelwood is an EP on the franchise, and the film's first-weekend viewership numbers will likely decide whether Amazon orders more.

Do I need to read the book first?

No. The film stands alone. But the book has the internal monologue, the side couple and roughly twice the plot — see reading order if the movie made you a convert.

Who plays Olive and Adam?

Lili Reinhart (Riverdale) plays Olive Smith; Tom Bateman (McMafia, Death on the Nile) plays Dr. Adam Carlsen. Reinhart also produces.

Is the movie rated R like the book?

No. The novel's explicit content was never going to survive a Prime Video launch intact; the film is positioned as young-adult friendly, with romance heat implied rather than shown.

Why does the book remind everyone of Star Wars?

Because it literally is one: Ali Hazelwood originally wrote the story as a Star Wars Reylo fanfiction before renaming the characters and publishing it as an original novel in 2021.
